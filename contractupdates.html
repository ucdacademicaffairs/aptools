Contract Provision Before (Prior Contracts) After (2026 Tentative) Administrative Impact
TA Appointment Percentages Departments had the flexibility to offer Teaching Assistant (TA) appointments at varying percentages (e.g., 25% FTE) based on budget availability and instructional needs. "When the University offers a Teaching Assistant (TA) or Associate Instructor appointment to an individual in a PhD and MFA program, the appointment will be at 50% FTE..." (Unless exceptions apply, such as combining with another appointment to equal 50%). Loss of Hiring Flexibility: Departments can no longer easily split funding to hire multiple students at 25% FTE. We can still notionally split appointments to hire the same student at 25% for one class, and 25% for another, to reach the 50% threshold. Anya wisely adds that needing to "double up" on lecture attendance hours would reduce the capcity of that "split" ASE for non-lecture work.
Reader Duties & Office Hours General definitions of Reader duties were sometimes stretched to include general course support, rarely including office hours. "A Reader... [is] employed to provide services which will normally include the grading of student assignments and examinations, and may include limited advising and demonstration work related to grading only. A Reader will not be given the other responsibilities customarily assigned to Teaching Assistants..." More Duty Separation: They still don't explicitly say it, for whatever reason, but the language has further evolved to prohibit Readers holding office hours. Econ and History are both pretty "safe" here, but it's something to be aware of.
Wages & Salary Scales Varying scales and historical campus tiers existed (e.g., disparities between "General" and "Special" scales, and different rates for different campuses). Unification of all salaried employees onto a single statewide scale by 2029, ending campus tiers. Continued annual salary increases until that point. Increased Labor Costs: The year-over-year differences are relatively modest on an individual scale. For example, over the next four years a given 50% TA would see their monthly salary increase by roughly $200-300 each year, step increase aside. At a college scale, however – it adds up, and might imply further budgetary pressure.
Childcare Reimbursements Systemwide reimbursement was capped at $1,400 per quarter (for ASEs). Immediate increase to $1,900 per quarter, scaling up to $2,050 by 2029. Applies across regular academic terms and summer sessions. Changing Benefit Overhead: It could be a nice point to stress with existing and prospective students. Increased fringe benefit costs could be a concern on a campus scale, and something to keep in mind when preparing grant applications, but from a departmental perspective we'd of course not be considering parental status as part of hiring decisions.
Workload & Class Sizes FTE-based workload average limits (110 hours per 25%) and hard caps (max 40 hours/week, max 8 hours/day). Existing workload caps are maintained, but there will apparently be a new process to discuss class sizes, per a side letter included in the new contract: systemwide Joint Labor-Management meetings, two per year, beginning 7/1/26, to discuss class sizes. Increased Scrutiny on Course Sizing: While workload remains effectively the same, we can expect increased scrutiny around class sizes. Hopefully more information on this is communicated soon...
International Worker Protections Pretty standard nondiscrimination protections. Limited specific leave provisions for immigration appointments. Creation of a $400,000 legal consultation fund. "The University will notify the union as soon as possible if it becomes aware of immigration officers on campus." Up to 3 weeks paid leave for immigration proceedings, and guaranteed rehiring if visa status is temporarily lost. New Compliance & Leave Burdens: We can expect increased administrative overhead in this area where leaves are concerned. Bolsters the Immigrant Legal Services Center, however, which is housed at UCD and is a good resource to have for current and prospective students.
Appointment Specificity Regarding the Description of Duties form, "it may include estimated time for effective completion of each duty." Still present! Retained Granularity: This is something that's actually not changed, but which has become an item of interest for one of our departments: the ability to specify for each ASE the amount of time anticipated for completion of each duty expected of them. An item on my list is to get answers from Grad Studies on whether their DOD template will be updated to include this info, now that we know that ability is sticking around.
* Information derived from the 2026 Mediator's Proposal and Tentative Agreements – as of this writing, not ratified by the union.
